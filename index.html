The scoring

Every stock in the Nifty Total Market list (the ~750 largest NSE companies) is scored on the same criteria each morning. Nothing is hand-picked, and the same inputs always produce the same ranking — you can check the arithmetic.

Must pass all of these first

    Why news can only remove, never promote

    Search results and headlines about stocks are saturated with promotional material — brokerage notes, paid articles, "5 stocks to buy today". A tool that ranked on news would rank whatever is being pushed hardest that morning, which is a reliable way to buy at the top.

    So headlines are used one way only: a name with a red flag in the last seven days — fraud allegation, auditor resignation, regulatory action, rating downgrade, promoter selling — is removed from the shortlist. Good news never lifts anything up the ranking.

    What the score cannot see

    • Whether management is honest and competent
    • Whether the accounts are truthful
    • Whether a competitor is about to destroy the business
    • Whether the recent numbers were a one-off
    • Pending litigation, tax demands or regulatory risk
    • Whether the company has any durable advantage at all

    Every one of those matters more than any ratio on this screen. All of them require reading the annual report. That is the actual work; this app only decides which report to read first.

    How to use this

    Open it in the morning. Look at rank 1 to 3. Then do nothing today.

    Instead, take one name and spend an evening on it: open the annual report on the company's website, read the Management Discussion and the auditor's report, and work out what the business actually does and who it competes with. Write down what you find. If after that you still like it, run it through the Investment Workbench, size the position properly, and only then consider buying.

    The gap between this shortlist and a purchase should be days, not minutes. The app exists to save you the search, not the thinking.

    What the numbers mean

    ROE — profit as a % of shareholders' money. Higher and steadier is better; above 20% is strong.
    ROA — profit as a % of all assets. Catches businesses that look good only because of leverage.
    OPM — operating profit as a % of sales. How much of each rupee of revenue survives costs.
    D/E — borrowed money versus owners' money. Below 0.5x is comfortable.
    CFO/PAT — cash from operations divided by reported profit. Below 0.7x for years is the single most useful accounting warning available to a retail investor.
    P/E — price divided by earnings per share. Only meaningful next to the same company's history and its sector.
    Earnings yield — the inverse of P/E, as a %. Comparable to a deposit rate, loosely.
    3-year range — where today's price sits between its 3-year low and high.

    Honest limitations

    • Data comes from a free, unofficial source. It has gaps and sometimes errors. Verify anything before acting on it.
    • Banks, NBFCs and insurers are excluded entirely — debt and cash-flow tests are meaningless for lenders.
    • The screen favours established, profitable, moderately priced companies. It will never find an early-stage winner, and it will sometimes rank a business in slow decline that merely looks statistically cheap. That is the known weakness of this approach.
    • Holding companies and cyclical commodity businesses often score well for reasons the score misreads. Check what a company actually does before believing its rank.
    • Prices shown are delayed. Check your broker app for live prices.
